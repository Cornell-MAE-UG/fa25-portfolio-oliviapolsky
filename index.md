## About Me


![Profile Picture]({{ "assets/images/IMG_3712.JPG" | relative_url }}){: class="profile-image"}

 
I'm Olivia Polsky, a junior studying Mechanical Engineering at Cornell University. This summer I worked as a Field Intern at Hunter Roberts Construction Group on a hospital renovation in Brooklyn, splitting my time between the field and office: managing submittals and RFIs, and tracking linear footage and manpower to help optimize production schedules.

What actually drives me is owning a problem from the moment it's undefined to the moment it's solved. On Cornell's Concrete Canoe team, I lead recruitment and help build out the next generation of the team. Earlier in my mechanical engineering coursework, I helped design and build No Fly Zone, a spotted lanternfly trap developed for Cornell CALS Extension, E&J Gallo Winery, and National Grape, taking it from concept through a working deliverable for real external clients.

That same instinct is what pulled me toward product. I noticed my roommates and I had no good system for splitting grocery runs, so I wrote the PRD, ran discovery interviews, and built Common Cart, a roommate expense splitting app, from a working React Native prototype. When I saw the ice cream shop I work at running custom cake orders off paper tickets, I built a full order tracking system with SMS notifications and photo scan intake, without waiting to be asked. I'm drawn to the moment a messy, unowned problem turns into something people can actually use.

I'm also exploring the space where engineering meets people, currently looking into human robot interaction and picking up a Psychology minor alongside my ME degree, because understanding how people actually behave feels just as important to good design as the technical side.

Outside of engineering, I play field hockey, play guitar, draw and paint, and I'm slowly building a small side project in sustainable fashion.
